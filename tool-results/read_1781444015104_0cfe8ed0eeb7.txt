Marketing loves them because we users love shiny things. You can even find <a href=\"https://dribbble.com/tags/hero-video\" target=\"_blank\" rel=\"noopener\">collections of examples all over the Internet</a>…</p>\n<figure id=\"attachment_10198\" class=\"wp-caption aligncenter\"><img fetchpriority=\"high\" decoding=\"async\" width=\"1899\" height=\"719\" class=\"size-full wp-image-10198\" src=\"https://aarontgrogg.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/video-hero-components-examples.png\" alt=\"Several examples of hero component layouts\" srcset=\"https://aarontgrogg.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/video-hero-components-examples.png 1899w, https://aarontgrogg.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/video-hero-components-examples-680x257.png 680w, https://aarontgrogg.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/video-hero-components-examples-1024x388.png 1024w, https://aarontgrogg.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/video-hero-components-examples-150x57.png 150w, https://aarontgrogg.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/video-hero-components-examples-768x291.png 768w, https://aarontgrogg.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/video-hero-components-examples-1536x582.png 1536w\" sizes=\"(max-width: 1899px) 100vw, 1899px\"><figcaption class=\"wp-caption-text\">Screenshot of video hero examples found on <a href=\"https://dribbble.com/tags/hero-video\" target=\"_blank\" rel=\"noopener\">Dribbble.com</a></figcaption></figure>\n<p>And I must admit, a nice splashy video at the top of the page, as the first thing you see, really does catch one’s attention! Except when it isn’t there…</p>\n<h2 id=\"toc\"><a href=\"#toc\"><abbr title=\"Table of Contents\">TOC</abbr></a></h2>\n<ul>\n<li><a href=\"#problem\">The Problem</a></li>\n<li><a href=\"#attempt\">An Attempted Solution</a></li>\n<li><a href=\"#solution\">The Responsive Solution</a></li>\n<li><a href=\"#comparison\">Comparison</a></li>\n<li><a href=\"#demo\">Demo</a></li>\n<li><a href=\"#enhancements\">Optional Enhancements</a></li>\n<li><a href=\"#challenges\">Challenges</a></li>\n<li><a href=\"#hope\">Future Hope?</a></li>\n<li><a href=\"#resources\">Resources</a></li>\n</ul>\n<h2 id=\"problem\"><a href=\"#problem\">The Problem</a></h2>\n<p>There are several challenges with using videos at the top of the page, including:</p>\n<ul class=\"inline-top\">\n<li>Videos typically take longer than images to download, because they tend to be larger files.</li>\n<li>Videos cannot start playing until a certain amount has downloaded.</li>\n<li>Videos cannot be preloaded, like images can be, so the browser doesn’t even know it needs to&nbsp;<em>start</em> downloading them until it sees the video element within the HTML.</li>\n<li>Designers like to position promotional text&nbsp;<em>over</em> the video which can result in something like white text on a white background…</li>\n</ul>\n<p>So until the browser finds the video element and downloads enough to start playing, there is nothing for the user to see. They just sit there, staring at that big old empty space, wondering whether something is happening, or whether something is broken…</p>\n<p>And don’t even get me started on mobile users, using data connections, trying to download that big fat shiny video of yours…</p>\n<p>Luckily, when this issue presents itself, it does so not only to the user, but also in our analytics! (You&nbsp;<em><strong>are</strong></em> using, and checking, your real-user monitoring (RUM), right?) Specifically, in this case, as a higher-than-normal Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) and, depending how the video player is added to the page, it could also be seen in a higher-than-normal Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS).</p>\n<p>In <a href=\"https://aarontgrogg.com/lab/video-hero-components/no-picture.html\" target=\"_blank\" rel=\"noopener\">this <em>very light</em> example page</a>, the portrait view recorded an LCP of ~3.7s and the landscape ~3.4s; CLS recorded ~0.14 and ~0.07, respectively:<br>\n<video style=\"max-height: 400px;\" src=\"https://aarontgrogg.com/lab/video-hero-components/video-hero-components-portrait-no-image.mp4\" preload=\"none\" muted=\"\" controls=\"controls\" playsinline=\"\" width=\"368\" height=\"840\"></video><br>\n<video style=\"max-height: 400px;\" src=\"https://aarontgrogg.com/lab/video-hero-components/video-hero-components-landscape-no-image.mp4\" preload=\"none\" muted=\"\" controls=\"controls\" playsinline=\"\" width=\"968\" height=\"722\"></video></p>\n<aside class=\"inline-top\">Notes:<p></p>\n<ul class=\"inline-top\">\n<li>I throttled to Slow 4G for testing because the page is so light.</li>\n<li>You could set an <code class=\"hljs\">aspect-ratio</code> for each responsive video size to resolve the CLS, but you would then also need to sync the breakpoints across the HTML and CSS.</li>\n</ul>\n</aside>\n<p><small><a title=\"Jump to the Table of Contents\" href=\"#toc\">TOC ⇪</a></small></p>\n<h2 id=\"attempt\"><a href=\"#attempt\">An Attempted Solution</a></h2>\n<p>Some of you keen observers may be saying “just use a <code class=\"hljs\">poster</code>, numbskull!” First of all, that is not nice. Second of all, <code class=\"hljs\">poster</code>s <em>do</em> work well, except in two common situations:</p>\n<ol class=\"inline-top\">\n<li>Responsive videos (when you need different aspect-ratio videos, depending on the screen size): The <code class=\"hljs\">poster</code> attribute/value can only be set on the <code class=\"hljs\">video</code> element, <em>not</em> on any <code class=\"hljs\">source</code> elements <em>within</em> the <code class=\"hljs\">video</code> element. This means you cannot have a different <code class=\"hljs\">poster</code> image for each different video aspect-ratio (i.e. portrait versus landscape layouts). In this case, users would always get one or the other poster image, regardless of their device’s screen size.</li>\n<li>Below-the-fold (not visible on the initial page load): If your video component is below-the-fold, you <em>can</em> lazy load the video asset (using <code class=\"hljs javascript\">preload=<span class=\"hljs-string\">\"none\"</span></code> on the <code class=\"hljs\">video</code> element), but you <em>can not</em> lazy load the <code class=\"hljs\">poster</code> asset attached to that <code class=\"hljs\">video</code> element; it will download as soon as the browser sees it, which could delay another, more critical asset from downloading, and may even be completely unnecessary to download if the user doesn’t scroll down far enough to see it.</li>\n</ol>\n<p>So, if you only have a single video asset that you want to serve for all device sizes, and it will always be above-the-fold, then a single <code class=\"hljs\">video</code> element with a <code class=\"hljs\">poster</code> attribute/value will work fine. Otherwise…</p>\n<p><small><a title=\"Jump to the Table of Contents\" href=\"#toc\">TOC ⇪</a></small></p>\n<h2 id=\"solution\"><a href=\"#solution\">The Responsive Solution</a></h2>\n<p>If you have multiple <em>video</em> aspect-ratios, then you need multiple <em>image</em> aspect-ratio. And the easiest way to do that, is by using the <em>picture</em> element!</p>\n<p>By creating a <code class=\"hljs\">picture</code> &gt; <code class=\"hljs\">source</code> structure that matches the <code class=\"hljs\">video</code> &gt; <code class=\"hljs\">source</code> structure, you can use CSS <code class=\"hljs\">grid</code> to layer them over one another. Specifically, layer the <code class=\"hljs\">video</code> <em>over</em> the <code class=\"hljs\">picture</code>.</p>\n<p>To the user, they will see the image first (because it will download faster than the video), and when the video downloads enough to start playing, it will do so <em>over</em> the image. The effect is especially powerful if the image is the first frame of the video; in this way, the image sort of “comes to life”…</p>\n<p>Using the same example page from above, but this time <a like this:</p>\n<pre class=\"inline-top css hljs\"><span class=\"hljs-selector-class\">.container--media-content</span> {\n  <span class=\"hljs-attribute\">display</span>: grid;\n  <span class=\"hljs\">media-content</code>.</li>\n<li>The existing grid and the new content container become its grid items, and are set to vertically-align <code class=\"hljs\">center</code> and horizontally-align left, but you can obviously do as you wish… ;-)</li>\n<li>The new container’s grid children are mapped to the <code class=\"hljs\">media-content</code> grid.</li>\n<li>I had to give the content container a <code class=\"hljs\">position: relative</code> to keep it accessible to clicks; perhaps there is a better way to do this?</li>\n</ul>\n</aside>\n<h3 id=\"below-the-fold\"><a href=\"#below-the-fold\">Below-the-Fold</a></h3>\n<p>If the video component will be “below-the-fold” (initially offscreen, not visible to the user when the page first loads), then you will <strong>not</strong> want to use any <code class=\"hljs\">preload</code> link or <code class=\"hljs\">fetchpriority</code> attribute, and you <strong>will</strong> want to lazy load all of the image and video assets.</p>\n<p>The resulting HTML would look something like this:</p>\n<pre class=\"inline-top html hljs xml\"><span class=\"hljs-tag\">&lt;<span class=\"hljs-name\">div</span> <span class=\"hljs-string\">\"720\"</span>\n            <span class=\"hljs-attr\">srcset</span>=<span class=\"hljs-string\">\"/path/to/landscape/image.webp\"</span> \n            <span <code class=\"hljs\">source</code> elements now have <code class=\"hljs javascript\">loading=<span class=\"hljs-string\">\"lazy\"</span></code> attributes/values, and the <code class=\"hljs\">fetchpriority</code> attribute has been removed.</li>\n<li>Both <code class=\"hljs\">video</code> <code class=\"hljs\">source</code> elements now have <code class=\"hljs javascript\">preload=<span class=\"hljs-string\">\"none\"</span></code> attributes/values, and the <code class=\"hljs\">fetchpriority</code> attribute has been removed.</li>\n<li>In case the <code class=\"hljs\">video</code> should automatically start playing as it scrolls into view, I have replaced the native <code class=\"hljs\">autoplay</code> attribute with a <code class=\"hljs\">data-autoplay</code> attribute. You would then also need to create an <a href=\"https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Intersection_Observer_API\" target=\"_blank\">IntersectionObserver</a> to monitor the element’s position in the browser and trigger the <code class=\"hljs\">play()</code>; that is beyond this article’s scope, but I would be happy to help if needed.</li>\n</ul>\n<p class=\"inline-top\">The CSS for this remains unchanged.</p>\n</aside>\n<p><small><a title=\"Jump to the Table of Contents\" href=\"#toc\">TOC ⇪</a></small></p>\n<h2 id=\"challenges\"><a href=\"#challenges\">Challenges</a></h2>\n<p>For the <em>most</em> part, I feel like this is a pretty simple, elegant component, considering how versatile and robust it is. But, of course, nothing is perfect…</p>\n<ul>\n<li>As I mentioned above, this layout is most effective when the image for each video is the first video frame. This isn’t <em>hard</em> to do, but it isn’t easy either, especially at scale. At one point, I began looking into how to “scrape” the first frame when the video is uploaded to a CMS, but the CMS my work was using at the time wasn’t going to allow this, so I moved on. It could still be an elegant solution…</li>\n<li>But then you would also need a way of “connecting” the video and the image in the CMS, to make sure that, as you add a video to a page, you also get the correct image for each version of each image… Naming conventions could help, but again, at scale, this could easily fall apart.</li>\n<li>Beyond syncing the correct image with the correct video, there is also a lot of syncing happening within the HTML, which might not always be in the same file/template:\n<ul class=\"inline-top inline-bottom\">\n<li>If you are using <code class=\"hljs\">preload</code> links, the URLs for the images you add to each page need to be added to the <code class=\"hljs\">preload</code> links, which need to go into the <code class=\"hljs\">head</code> element.</li>\n<li>If you are using <code class=\"hljs\">preload</code> links, the breakpoints for the images and videos within the content need to be synced with the breakpoints in the <code class=\"hljs\">preload</code> links. Ideally, this is a one-time, set-it-and-forget-it template update, but different layouts across different pages/templates could require different breakpoints.</li>\n<li>The image and video assets need to be the exact same width and height, and those values have to be included in the <code class=\"hljs\">source</code> elements of both the <code class=\"hljs\">picture</code> and <code class=\"hljs\">video</code> elements.</li>\n<li>If your page components can either migrate up and down the page, or be used anywhere within the page, considerations need to be made to deal with the correct autoplay, lazy load (and maybe <code class=\"hljs\">preload</code> link) settings.</li>\n<li>If a video <strong>should</strong> autoplay, but it appears below the fold, know that currently there is no native way for a video to be lazy loaded <strong><em>and</em></strong> autoplay when it scrolls into view; to do this, you would need to convert the <code class=\"hljs\">autoplay</code> to something like a <code class=\"hljs\">data-autoplay</code>, then have JS look for that, using an IntersectionObserver to monitor when it gets close to the viewport, and trigger the autoplay programmatically.</li>\n</ul>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>Some of the above are also issues <em>outside</em> of this component, but I wanted to make sure they were stated.</p>\n<p><small><a title=\"Jump to the Table of Contents\" href=\"#toc\">TOC ⇪</a></small></p>\n<h2 id=\"hope\"><a href=\"#hope\">Future Hope?</a></h2>\n<p>There is hope on the horizon for some of those challenges <a href=\"https://github.com/whatwg/html/issues/10376\" target=\"_blank\" rel=\"noopener\">being discussed by some really smart people</a>.</p>\n<p>With luck, all of this could be solved some day by an adaptation to a standard.</p>\n<p><small><a title=\"Jump to the Table of Contents\" href=\"#toc\">TOC ⇪</a></small></p>\n<h2 id=\"resources\"><a href=\"#resources\">Resources</a></h2>\n<p>I doubt very much that I am the first to think about this, but I am documenting my process nonetheless.</p>\n<p>I was heavily influenced by the following:</p>\n<ul class=\"inline-top\">\n<li><span class=\"vcard\" vocab=\"http://schema.org/\" typeof=\"Person\" itemscope=\"\" itemtype=\"https://schema.org/Person\"><a class=\"url\" property=\"url\" itemprop=\"url\" href=\"https://scottjehl.com\" rel=\"v:url\"><span class=\"fn n\" property=\"name\" itemprop=\"name\"><span class=\"given-name\" property=\"givenName\" itemprop=\"givenName\">Scott</span> <span class=\"family-name\" property=\"familyName\" itemprop=\"familyName\">Jehl</span></span></a></span>‘s <a href=\"https://scottjehl.com/posts/rwd-video/\">Responsive Video Works Now.
